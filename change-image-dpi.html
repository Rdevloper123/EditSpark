What is Image DPI and Why is 300 DPI Important?

DPI stands for Dots Per Inch. It dictates how many dots of ink a printer places within a linear inch of a physical document. In digital platforms, it acts as a layout metadata layer telling hardware tools how to scale an image during print output. While mobile screens depend completely on pixel width and height, physical printing and scanners require correct embedded values to avoid a blurry output.

Using a photo DPI converter to set your asset to 300 DPI image format is highly recommended because it compresses pixel clusters. This density ensures that individual grids remain invisible to the naked human eye, resulting in clean, razor-sharp documents.
