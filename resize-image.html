What is 300 DPI?

DPI means Dots Per Inch, which is basically a measure of how clear and crisp an image will look when it gets printed on paper. While mobile screens and computer monitors show pictures nicely at 72 DPI, printing machines need much higher detail. If your image has a low DPI setting, it might look blurry or stretched out when printed on an identity card or application sheet. That is why high resolution is crucial.

When to Use 300 DPI

You need to convert image to 300 dpi whenever you are filling out official online registration forms. Government job applications, college admissions, passport offices, and visa portals require this exact setting. It ensures that when officers print out your application form, your face and signature look sharp and perfectly legible.

Resize by Pixels vs Percentage

Our tool gives you two ways to adjust your image dimensions easily. If you have exact instructions from an exam notification, you can use Pixels to enter the exact width and height you need.
